# Reader-Acquisition SEO Roadmap

The 43 launch pages establish the Ateshi entity graph, serve existing readers and convert branded interest. They are not expected to create large non-brand search demand by themselves.

The next SEO layer should publish genuinely useful reader-discovery articles under `/blog/`. These posts must earn their place for readers who have never heard of *The Ashen Flame*.

## Strategic separation

| Layer | Job | Primary measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Lore and entity pages | Explain the fictional world and retain interested readers | Indexing, branded impressions, exploration depth, assisted book visits |
| Reader-acquisition articles | Win existing genre and comparison demand | Non-brand impressions, qualified entrances, book-page assists |
| Craft and methodology articles | Earn links and reach writers/worldbuilders | Referring domains, citations, newsletter assists |

Do not force search-volume phrases into canonical lore pages. Acquisition content should link into the entity graph where it genuinely helps the reader.

## Selection methodology for book lists

Every recommendation article must state:

1. the qualities being compared;
2. which editions or publication window were considered;
3. that Alexei Sassani is the author of *The Ashen Flame*;
4. why the author's book is included, without presenting self-interest as an independent award;
5. the date the list was checked or updated.

The list must recommend other books substantively. It cannot exist as a disguised landing page with one real recommendation and the author's title.

## Page requirements

Every blog article needs the same content contract as a lore page:

- unique canonical intent;
- final copy and visible disclosure;
- `pageType: blog-post`, `section: blog`, `schemaType: Article`;
- title, description and spoiler-safe social metadata;
- author, publication and modification dates;
- methodology or sourcing where relevant;
- contextual links into the Ateshi archive;
- a restrained book CTA;
- image brief and social crops;
- no invented review scores, sales claims or awards.

## Publishing sequence

Publish the first two alternate-history pieces and one craft article before adding **Blog** to primary navigation. Until then, discovery articles can be reached from the homepage or footer without an empty hub.

Recommended cadence after launch:

- Weeks 1–2: alternate-history cornerstone and genre guide;
- Week 3: fictional-religion craft article;
- Week 4: first comparison article;
- Thereafter: one acquisition or link-earning article per week, with one lore/archive release between every two commercial posts.

## Refresh policy

- Review commercial lists quarterly for availability, new releases and broken retailer links.
- Refresh year-based pages before the year begins and again after major publishing seasons.
- Preserve the URL when the search intent remains the same; redirect only when consolidating genuinely overlapping pages.
- Record substantive update dates, not cosmetic timestamp changes.

## Measurement and attribution

Track at minimum:

- Google Search Console query and page performance by content layer;
- non-brand entrances to `/blog/`;
- article-to-book and article-to-lore clicks;
- extract views and verified retailer outbound clicks;
- newsletter sign-ups by landing page;
- referring domains to maps, timelines and craft pieces;
- assisted conversions rather than last-click sales alone.

Do not declare that the site “dominates” because fictional names rank first. Success requires qualified non-brand discovery, defensible links, reader engagement and measurable movement towards the book.
