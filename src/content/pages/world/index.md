> **Fiction notice:** The Ateshi world is an alternate setting created for *The Ashen Flame*. It uses real geographical names within invented political and religious history.

The Ateshi world stretches from Persia and the Indus through Central Asia and the Caucasus into Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova and the Baltic states. Its modern states are connected through the Compact, a shared religious inheritance and Common Persian as a language of institutions.

They are not one culture.

Local languages, political systems, memories of conquest and relationships with Ateshism differ across the map. A Compact diagram can make the arrangement look inevitable and uniform. The story repeatedly shows that neither impression is true.

## The Compact

[The Compact](/world/compact/) connects Ateshi-majority member states through movement, trade and recognised professional standards. A doctor trained in Kyiv can take a hospital post in Shiraz without repeating an entire qualification. Machinery can cross several borders with one set of papers.

Integration creates arguments as efficiently as opportunities. Russian industrial standards affect Persian firms. Northern governments question Persian influence. Oil, roads and professional recognition produce winners, costs and paperwork.

The Compact is therefore a political arrangement, not another name for the faith or for a surviving empire.

## Persia

[Persia](/world/persia/) is Farah Kianzad's country and one of the Ateshi world's principal political, religious and economic centres. Tehran is its capital. Isfahan is a major city of universities, Temple investment and historic infrastructure.

Persia is a constitutional monarchy: Parliament governs, while the Shah represents continuity and ceremony. The Temple cannot legally overturn civil government but has immense influence through institutions and accumulated wealth.

Persian importance can be useful and resented. A citizen from Belarus may criticise Persian weight inside the Compact and still defend Ateshism to an outsider. Political disagreement does not require a single cultural identity.

## Khorasan

Khorasan is Persia's northeastern highland province, extending from central deserts towards the Kopet Dag and historic routes into Central Asia. It is not an independent country, and Farah crosses no international border when she takes her first posting there.

Tus is the provincial capital. Dargaz, a northern mountain town, becomes Farah's professional home. Khorasan's languages, devotional culture and distance from Tehran shape the Anousheh investigation.

People in the province can regard central oversight as necessary, arrogant or both. The statement that Anousheh brought clinics “when Tehran sent promises” captures the political force of practical care.

## Central Asia

East of Persia, school maps show:

- **Sogdiana**, centred around Samarkand and Bukhara;
- **Bactria**, beyond the upper Oxus;
- **Khwarazm**, along its lower waters;
- **Turkmenia**, south of Khwarazm;
- **Kazakhstan** and **Kyrgyzstan**, farther north and east.

Uzbek and Tajik remain peoples and languages, but Uzbekistan and Tajikistan do not exist as states in this timeline. Afghanistan begins south of the Hindu Kush and is smaller than on real-world maps because Balkh and much of the northern plain belong to Bactria.

The Anousheh referral enters Farah's office from the Samarkand district in Sogdiana, making Compact jurisdiction and Persian influence part of the case from its beginning.

## The Caucasus and Ararat

The Compact includes the Ateshi-majority Caucasian republics of Azerbaijan, Armenia, Georgia, Circassia, Alania and Dagestan.

The Republic of Ararat is an independent Ateshi state centred on Van in eastern Anatolia. Turkey begins to its west. Ararat's existence reflects an alternate political history that should not be confused with present real-world borders.

Regional pages will examine how the shared faith meets Armenian, Georgian, Azeri, Circassian, Alanian, Dagestani and Anatolian memory rather than treating “the Caucasus” as a single culture.

## The northern states

Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania belong to the Compact in Farah's lifetime. Their incorporation into the historical Ateshi sphere involved conquest and generations of institutional change.

Northern members preserve distinct languages, national sports, political arguments and historical names for imperial events. A hockey result can reveal regional identity faster than an official treaty.

The modern religious majority should not be read backwards as proof that conquest was gentle or conversion immediate.

## Pakistan and Afghanistan

Pakistan and Afghanistan are Compact members, but their position near substantial Muslim communities and neighbouring Muslim-majority states gives religion and diplomacy a different public character from northern Europe.

Persia's own Muslim citizens participate in civil government and professional life while navigating Ateshi-majority institutions. The novel resists speaking of “the Muslim world” as though many countries and communities share one mind.

## Common Persian and household languages

Every Compact school teaches Common Persian as a shared second language. Scripture, authoritative rulings, interregional prayer, government and Ash records use it across borders.

Behind dormitory doors and inside homes, other languages return: Azeri, Uzbek, Tajik Persian, Belarusian, Ukrainian, Russian and many more. A person may conduct official work in Persian and insist that the best gossip cannot be trusted to it.

Language is also institutional power. Native fluency can make one person's hesitation look like uncertainty when another is simply translating thought into the official tongue.

## Travel and material life

The Compact is visible in railways, standards, food and work rather than flags alone. Persian fuel moves north while timber and machinery travel south. Trains serve Persian tea beside Belarusian rye and bottled Sindhi mango drinks. Japanese and German technology shares streets with domestic and northern manufacturing.

Integration produces hybrid foods no province can assign cleanly. Grandmothers treat this less as geopolitics than as other people ruining recipes.
