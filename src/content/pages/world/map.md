> **Fiction notice:** This page maps an invented alternate world. It reuses real geographical names but does not depict real present-day political borders.

The map of the Ateshi world shows independent states connected through the Compact, not one surviving empire. Persia occupies the historical centre of much of the faith, while member territories extend east through Central and South Asia and northwest through the Caucasus, Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova and the Baltic.

The finished visual map will be added as a verified asset. This text version contains the same essential political information so geography remains accessible without an image.

## Core orientation

| Region | Political geography in the Ateshi world | Story relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Persia | Sovereign constitutional monarchy; Tehran is capital | Farah's country and childhood home |
| Khorasan | Northeastern province of Persia | Farah's first posting and main investigation region |
| Sogdiana | Central Asian Compact republic around Samarkand and Bukhara | Source of the Anousheh referral |
| Bactria | Republic beyond the upper Oxus; includes Balkh and much northern plain | Shapes Afghanistan's alternate northern border |
| Khwarazm | Republic along the lower Oxus | Part of the Central Asian Compact corridor |
| Turkmenia | Republic south of Khwarazm | Links Persian and Central Asian routes |
| Ararat | Independent Ateshi republic centred on Van | Marks the Compact's eastern Anatolian edge |
| Caucasus | Azerbaijan, Armenia, Georgia, Circassia, Alania and Dagestan | Distinct member republics, not a single culture |
| Northern members | Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania | Ateshi-majority states with separate languages and memories |
| South Asian members | Pakistan and Afghanistan | Compact states bordering large Muslim populations and neighbours |

## Persia and Khorasan

[Persia](/world/persia/) sits between the Gulf, the Zagros, Central Asia and South Asia. Tehran is its capital; Isfahan is a major university and institutional centre.

Khorasan runs from Persia's central deserts towards the Kopet Dag and the old caravan routes. Tus is its provincial capital. Dargaz lies in its northern mountain country.

The map must keep Khorasan inside Persia's national boundary. Earlier worldbuilding sometimes treated regional names as countries; the publication master is explicit that Farah crosses no international border to reach her posting.

## Central Asia

The principal republics named in the novel are Sogdiana, Bactria, Khwarazm, Turkmenia, Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan.

Samarkand and Bukhara sit within Sogdiana. Bactria lies beyond the upper Oxus and includes territory that alters Afghanistan's familiar outline. Khwarazm follows the lower river, with Turkmenia below it.

Uzbek and Tajik identify peoples and languages. **Uzbekistan and Tajikistan are not states** in this timeline; school materials remember them as unsuccessful nationalist proposals. The visual map must not import contemporary real-world boundaries automatically.

## The Caucasus and Anatolia

The Ateshi-majority Caucasian republics are individually represented: Azerbaijan, Armenia, Georgia, Circassia, Alania and Dagestan.

Ararat is centred on Van in eastern Anatolia. Turkey begins west of it. The map should label the state clearly enough that readers do not mistake Ararat for a province of Persia or a general name for the Armenian highlands.

## Russia, Ukraine, Belarus and the Baltic

The Compact continues through Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania. Each remains a state with its own government, culture and language.

Shared Ateshi majority does not erase the violence of earlier expansion. A map is especially capable of disguising that history by making borders look natural. Historical layers should eventually distinguish modern membership from the routes of conquest and conversion.

## Pakistan and Afghanistan

Pakistan and Afghanistan are member states. Afghanistan's northern boundary differs from the familiar real-world map because Balkh and most of the northern plain belong to Bactria.

The southern and western edges of the Compact meet Muslim-majority states and regions with their own histories of trade, conflict and migration. The political map should show neighbours without recolouring them as an undifferentiated outside world.

## What the map should display

The primary political map requires:

1. sovereign state boundaries;
2. the Compact's full extent through a restrained overlay;
3. Persia's provincial boundary around Khorasan;
4. national capitals and a smaller class of story locations;
5. seas, major rivers and mountain systems required for orientation;
6. a legend explicitly labelling the setting as fictional alternate history;
7. accessible HTML text equivalent to every essential map distinction.

The map should not imply that every area within a member state is uniformly Ateshi or culturally identical.

## Story locations

The first story layer should include:

- **Tehran** — Farah's childhood home;
- **Isfahan** — university and Ash formation context;
- **Tus** — Khorasan's provincial capital and rail connection;
- **Dargaz** — Farah's first Ash posting;
- **Samarkand** — the Sogdian district from which the referral emerges;
- **Tabriz** — a city connected with Anousheh's early formation history.

Location pins must not reveal S2 or S3 plot information in labels or previews.

## Future map layers

Later maps can show historical expansion, languages, pilgrimage and story journeys. Each layer needs its own permanent explanation and should not create indexable parameter variations.

The political map owns the canonical geographical overview. Historical articles should embed or reference it rather than publishing conflicting boundaries.
