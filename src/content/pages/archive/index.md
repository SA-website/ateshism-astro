> **Fiction notice:** Every object in the Ash Archive is created for the fictional world of *The Ashen Flame*. No item should be represented as an authentic real-world historical document.

The **Ash Archive** is the documentary collection within Ateshism.com: maps, photographs, notices, letters, institutional records, transcripts and fragments that make the Ateshi world available through objects rather than encyclopedia articles alone.

It is a reader-facing editorial archive, not a claim that every item belongs to one literal in-world repository. Individual records identify their fictional creator, date, place, collection context and degree of canon authority.

## What belongs in the archive

The collection will include:

- Ash files, forms and training records;
- Temple notices and Rose circulars;
- fictional newspapers and public broadcasts;
- maps and transport documents;
- family letters, household papers and photographs;
- posters, advertisements and magazine covers;
- theological pamphlets and scholarly fragments;
- government memoranda and parliamentary material;
- museum-style reconstructions of older objects;
- audio transcripts and period media.

An archive item must add evidence, perspective or atmosphere. Decorative paper made to look old is not enough.

## The first collections

### Ash Order records

These objects show how investigators classify information and preserve institutional accountability. Planned items include Farah Kianzad's ordination record, an Ash examination paper, an investigation form and a tattoo ledger.

Ash documents must not reveal conclusions through filenames, image alt text or public metadata. Case material uses the same spoiler discipline as character pages.

### Temple life

Wednesday rotation notices, Common Ember declarations, clinic records and public-service posters show how religious institutions enter daily life. These objects are most useful when their ordinary administrative language exposes the relationship between care and power.

### Ateshi history

Historical layers can include copied sayings, disputed manuscript fragments, institutional catalogues and later accounts of conquest. Each item must distinguish a period source from a modern reconstruction.

A Temple chronicle and a Belarusian account of the same event can appear together without the archive declaring one culturally neutral.

### The Anousheh record

Public, premise-safe objects may include devotional portraits, clinic appeals and contemporary coverage already visible near the beginning of the novel. The complete Anousheh dossier remains on hold because its contents would reveal S2 and S3 material.

No “mystery clue” should be placed into search results merely to make the archive feel interactive.

## Object records and provenance

Every archive page uses a consistent description:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Object title | Clear and unique, without leaking spoilers |
| Fictional date | Exact only where canon supports it |
| Creator | Person or institution represented as creating the object |
| Place | Jurisdiction or location relevant to production |
| Collection | Reader-facing grouping within the Ash Archive |
| Format | Letter, photograph, form, map, audio or other medium |
| Transcription | Accessible text equivalent where applicable |
| Archivist's note | Context, uncertainty and relationship to the novel |
| Canon status | Editorial field controlling publication |
| Spoiler level | S0–S3, applied to metadata as well as body content |

Fictional provenance is part of the worldbuilding. The permanent fiction notice prevents that detail from becoming deception.

## Photographs

The visual language is documentary rather than painterly. Images should look as though a camera in the relevant period could have made them: credible optics, available light, natural grain, correct vehicles, technology, clothing and signage.

The archive spans more than one year. A 1991 photograph must be accurate to 1991; an image from the 2010 investigation must not be forced into the same material period. Neither should use fantasy glow, artificial skin texture or generic religious architecture.

Portrait consistency matters. Farah, Anousheh, Golnaz and recurring characters cannot change face from one file to the next.

## Maps

The [map of the Ateshi world](/world/map/) is the first major archival asset. It will establish the political relationship among Persia, Khorasan, Central Asia, the Caucasus, Ararat and the northern Compact states.

Later layers can show historical expansion, languages and story travel. Every visual map needs an HTML equivalent so its essential facts remain available to screen readers and search engines.

## Spoiler control

S0 objects can be indexed. S1 objects may reveal only the novel's premise and early context. S2 and S3 objects must not be hidden only with client-side styling while remaining in page source.

Until a secure implementation exists, high-spoiler objects remain outside the public build. Search crawlers, link previews and internal search must never surface their text.

## Enter the archive newsletter

The newsletter promise is specific:

> Receive newly released documents, photographs and histories from the fictional world of *The Ashen Flame*.

Future releases might include a Temple notice, a recovered public interview, a map layer or a short history. Subscription should never be required to understand a core page, and no form should be connected until its provider, consent language and privacy policy are configured.
