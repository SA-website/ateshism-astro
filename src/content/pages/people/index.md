> **Fiction and spoiler notice:** Every person in this section is fictional. Character pages reveal only premise-level information by default; major discoveries and endings are excluded from indexable summaries.

The people of the Ateshi world live inside institutions and relationships larger than themselves without becoming simple examples of them. A Rose Mother can provide extraordinary care and still exercise unequal power. An investigator can value evidence and remain shaped by childhood. A family can love one another while failing to name what one member carries.

The first public dossiers focus on the two women at the centre of *The Ashen Flame*: **Farah Kianzad**, who conducts the investigation, and **Mother Anousheh**, whose work, relationships and reputation become its subject.

## Farah Kianzad

[Farah Kianzad](/people/farah-kianzad/) grows up in an outer neighbourhood of Tehran. At nine, she counts broken courtyard tiles while her family tries to describe a new Sister-Bond as though everyone must experience it in the same way.

She becomes an Ash Mother: an investigator trained to separate fact, testimony, inference, assumption and unknown. Her first posting is Dargaz in northern Khorasan. In spring 2010, aged twenty-eight, she receives a referral concerning Anousheh.

Farah's strength is not certainty. It is her unwillingness to let the number of pages in a file frighten her into believing they contain an answer.

## Mother Anousheh

[Mother Anousheh](/people/anousheh/) is a senior Rose Mother in her fifties, prominent across Khorasan and Sogdiana. Supporters call her Keeper of the Eastern Hearth and carry her image into births, examinations, military departures and hospitals.

Her career includes genuine institutional achievement. She takes over a failing maternal clinic, secures staff and funding, and helps lower mortality. She remembers names, injuries, examinations and old jokes. Her attention makes people feel recognised.

The Ash referral concerns whether pastoral, professional and intimate roles overlapped without adequate disclosure. It does not begin from the assumption that good work was fraudulent or that multiple marriages were an offence.

## Mehr Arahita

[Mehr Arahita](/faith/mehr-arahita/) is the ninth-century First Witness associated with the founding of Ateshism. After the death of an infant daughter, she experiences the First Burning beside a household fire.

Arahita is not worshipped as an incarnation or goddess. Her sayings form the *Book of Fire*, while the attribution of the later *Book of the Hidden Flame* remains disputed.

She belongs to religious history rather than the novel's modern character list, but every major institution claims some relationship to her memory.

## Farah's family

Farah's parents are **Behnoosh** and **Omid Kianzad**. Behnoosh's Sister-Bond partner is **Parvin**.

Behnoosh believes her recognised bond can exist without diminishing her marriage. Omid does not claim the right to prohibit it, but struggles against a culture with little room for his grief and jealousy. Parvin is kind enough to make easy resentment impossible. Farah grows up listening to all three.

Their household is not backstory that disappears when the investigation begins. It is the place where Farah first learns that legality, sincere intention and emotional consequence can all be true at once.

## The Khorasan years

**Homa Darvishi** is the Senior Ash Mother for the Dargaz district and Farah's superior. Her guidance is procedural and exact: write access before coercion; investigate the person in the file rather than the saint or blasphemy other people want the case to become.

**Kaveh** meets Farah through work surrounding a water-rights dispute. Their Temporary Marriage teaches her that a loving relationship can be finite without becoming a failure.

These characters place the Anousheh case inside lived Khorasan rather than allowing Farah to arrive as a neutral representative of Tehran.

## Samarkand and the archive

**Golnaz** is the Samarkand Temple archivist who anticipates the records Farah will need. She tells Farah openly that she likes Anousheh and will still help the inquiry because records are records.

Her candour complicates Farah's habit of treating every emotional response as a possible investigative method. Golnaz is neither a neutral database nor a convenient guide. She belongs to the community whose records are being opened.
