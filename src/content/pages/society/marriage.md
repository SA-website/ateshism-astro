> **Fiction notice:** Ateshi marriage is an invented legal and religious system. It is not a guide to any real-world marriage law.

Ateshi law recognises relationships by the promises people choose to make, including a **Sacred Circle** without a fixed ending and a **Temporary Marriage** whose duration is declared in advance.

Both forms require capacity, recorded terms and explicit consent. Neither makes a relationship morally equal by definition. The law can document a promise; it cannot make every person equally able to refuse it.

## Sacred Circles

A Sacred Circle assumes continuity without claiming that dissolution is impossible. Its registry uses a deep red seal and does not begin with an expiry date.

The form is culturally associated with permanence, household formation and a public willingness to build an undetermined future together. That expectation can provide stability. It can also make ending the relationship feel like the failure of a sacred claim rather than the conclusion of a human one.

Dissolution rules formally protect equal division. Practice does not always follow the balance promised in statute. Ash training materials in the novel repeatedly show homes and children remaining with women after separation. The pattern emerges through decisions and expectations rather than an openly unequal sentence in law.

## Temporary Marriage

A Temporary Marriage uses an amber registry seal and places its expiry date on the first page. It may last months, years or a much shorter stated period. Renewal requires a new act of consent rather than an assumption that silence extends the relationship.

The form exists to give a finite partnership recognised terms and responsibilities. It is not required for casual sex: two adults do not need a marriage contract to enter a private consensual relationship. The distinction explains why the administrative record matters. Temporary Marriage is about a defined legal bond, not clerical permission for desire.

The petition must formally be initiated by the woman. The safeguard was designed to prevent men turning public proposals into pressure. It also creates an asymmetry: a man may ask, hint or hope, but he cannot make the recognised proposal. A rule can protect against one history of coercion while limiting another person's legitimate speech.

## What the contract records

The main agreement may be short. Its supporting medical and civil provisions are not. Records can address:

- the term and process for renewal;
- property and financial responsibility;
- health disclosures and recent tests;
- contraception and intentions concerning conception;
- parentage and duties surviving expiry;
- existing partners and overlapping households;
- confidentiality within the limits of law;
- procedures for complaint or early dissolution.

The detail reflects an Ateshi administrative principle expressed humorously in the novel: desire may be brief, but biology is administrative.

## Pregnancy and parenthood

A pregnancy does not automatically extend a Temporary Marriage. That rule protects the pregnant partner from having a relationship rewritten without consent. Parenthood, however, cannot expire with the contract.

The parties must address guardianship, residence, financial support and contact. Their original intention not to have children remains relevant history but cannot erase the obligations created by a child.

The system is clearest on legal duty and least capable of equalising imagination. One partner may begin to imagine permanence after a pregnancy while the other does not. A lawful ending can still create grief for which no procedural violation exists.

## Existing partners and plural households

Ateshi society recognises overlapping relationships, including [Sister-Bonds](/society/sister-bonds/) and other plural arrangements. Registry guidance requires existing partners to be considered rather than treated as invisible.

This does not give one adult ownership of another's decision. It does make disclosure and household consequence part of responsible consent. Rent, sleeping arrangements, inheritance, childcare and time do not disappear because a theology celebrates the abundance of love.

The strongest Ateshi defence of plural partnership is that love need not be scarce. Its strongest internal criticism is that time, money and emotional safety remain scarce even when love is not.

## Consent and institutional power

Marriage records preserve signatures and declarations. They cannot establish that every “yes” was equally free.

The problem becomes acute when a Rose Mother proposes a relationship to somebody she has counselled, housed, recommended or spiritually guided. Refusal may be legal while feeling personally, professionally or religiously costly. A conflict declaration does not solve the relationship, but its absence can conceal the roles operating around it.

This is the premise of the Anousheh inquiry in *The Ashen Flame*. Eleven Temporary Marriages are neither illegal nor the allegation. The investigation concerns overlap between intimacy and institutional influence.

## Ceremony and registration

The legal forms described here should be distinguished from the **Ateshi marriage ceremony**, which will have its own post-launch page. Communities vary in music, clothing, vows, household flame and celebration. The registry establishes civil and institutional recognition; ceremony gives a relationship public and devotional language.

Some adults choose registration with a small rite. Others hold a large gathering. A relationship's scale of celebration does not change the standard of consent.

## Dissolution and endings

Ateshi thought can recognise a finite relationship as complete rather than failed. A Temporary Marriage that ends on its agreed date may have been loving, serious and worthwhile. A Sacred Circle may dissolve without making every earlier promise fraudulent.

The ethical task is not to declare every ending successful. It is to judge the relationship by what people did, what they concealed, what obligations remain and whether either person used sacred language to make departure impossible.

Law supplies dates, signatures and procedures. Human beings still have to tell the truth about what those records could not contain.
