> **Fiction notice:** This chronology belongs to an invented alternate history. Approximate dates remain approximate where the publication master does not establish an exact year.

The public Ateshi timeline spans roughly twelve centuries, from Mehr Arahita's ninth-century community to the beginning of Farah Kianzad's investigation in 2010.

It is intentionally not exact at every point. A false year would look authoritative without becoming canon. The sequence below distinguishes fixed novel dates, securely ordered eras and events whose precise dating remains disputed.

## Ninth century: Arahita and the first community

Tradition places the [First Burning](/faith/mehr-arahita/) after the death of Arahita's infant daughter. The experience does not explain the loss. It changes Arahita's recognition of divine presence within embodied grief.

Her sayings are compiled during her lifetime as the [*Book of Fire—The Sayings of Arahita*](/faith/book-of-fire/). The earliest community keeps fires, boils water, carries food, assists families through birth and death, copies teachings and educates children.

No precise birth, death or revelation year is currently public canon. Future chronology should not backfill one from exploratory AI conversations.

## After Arahita: the houses remain open

After the First Witness dies, the community continues. Service becomes administration because kitchens need provisions, clinics need skills and donations need accounts.

Zarrindokht, remembered as the **Second Founder**, creates formation, provincial houses, ordination and records. Traditional history treats her as the woman who made continuity possible. Institutional critics identify the same work as the moment a household movement began becoming a government.

## The spread of Ateshism

Healers, merchants, teachers and marriages carry the faith along roads and across languages. Some regions encounter an Ateshi clinic before an Ateshi army.

The movement later militarises. In a line taught to Ash recruits, the first generation converts people and the third conquers them. The phrase establishes sequence rather than an exact regnal chronology.

Ateshi conquest includes sieges, taxation, rebellion and pressure towards conversion. It also establishes clinics, schools, water systems and legal reforms. Neither record erases the other.

## Roughly three centuries later: the Hidden Flame

The [*Book of the Hidden Flame*](/faith/book-of-hidden-flame/) becomes public approximately three centuries after Arahita. Tradition claims that a custodian lineage preserved teachings too dangerous or demanding for the early community to reveal.

The book develops the fifth pillar—Consent as Cosmic Principle—and supplies later architecture for embodiment, relationships and authority. Whether Arahita spoke its words remains unresolved. The date of unveiling is more secure than the attribution.

## Imperial centuries and conflicting memory

Ateshi political rule expands through Persia, Central Asia, the Caucasus and farther north and west. Local histories preserve events under names that reveal their position.

The Temple's “Liberation of the Hearths” in Belarus is the “Burning Summer” in Belarusian memory. Villages are destroyed; hereditary debt bondage is abolished. A responsible timeline records both rather than converting one into a verdict on the other.

Conquest is followed by generations of cultural change through law, education, patronage, marriage and religious service. Formal freedom of belief does not make the surrounding distribution of power neutral.

## From empire to Compact

By the modern era, Ateshi-majority territories exist as distinct states joined through [the Compact](/world/compact/). Professional qualifications, trade and movement can cross borders through shared frameworks. Common Persian serves government, education and interregional religious life.

The final chronology of the Compact's treaties, crises and constitutional stages remains under canon review. This page therefore records its modern function without inventing a founding year.

## 1991: Farah's first recorded year

In spring 1991, nine-year-old Farah lives in an outer Tehran neighbourhood. The radio mentions Kuwait between traffic, weather and music. Her mother Behnoosh enters a Sister-Bond with Parvin, changing the household and giving Farah her first sustained encounter with the difference between formal legitimacy and emotional consequence.

The [Ateshi world in 1991](/history/1991/) page places this family story within the period's government, media, technology and social arguments.

## 1999: university and the wider Compact

Farah travels by electric express from Tehran to Isfahan to visit Mehr University. The journey brings the Compact into view through trade, professional recognition, multilingual travellers and ordinary complaints about shared standards.

She later enters Rose foundational formation before moving into the separate Ash track.

## c. 2004: Dargaz

Farah's first Ash posting takes her to Dargaz in northern Khorasan. The year is inferred: by spring 2010 she is in her sixth year there. Treating 2004 as exact would overstate the text, so the date remains approximate.

## 2010: the Anousheh investigation begins

In spring 2010, Farah is twenty-eight. A referral from the Samarkand district office concerns overlaps between Mother Anousheh's Temporary Marriages and institutional files involving counsel, housing and appointments.

This is the novel's principal investigation period. Earlier versions of the website blueprint incorrectly labelled 1991 as that setting; the publication master establishes 2010.
